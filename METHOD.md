# Openwrought Method

Openwrought follows a three-stage cycle.

## Emberlight — surface the signal

Find questions, tensions, curiosities, disappearing knowledge, or unresolved practical problems that are producing a real public signal. Avoid manufacturing topics solely to maintain output.

## Aeon — establish the record

1. Define the exact question.
2. Identify primary or closest-available evidence.
3. Preserve provenance: source, date, context, and uncertainty.
4. Separate observation from inference.
5. Search for contradictory evidence.
6. Revise the hypothesis when contrary evidence appears.
7. Record unresolved uncertainty rather than smoothing it away.

## Loom — make it useful

Turn the verified record into something another person can use: a protocol, map, dataset, checklist, field guide, small tool, or experiment.

## Reality Check Protocol 001

Before an answer hardens into advice:

1. Identify the exact object or context.
2. Find primary evidence.
3. Separate observation from inference.
4. Choose a reversible test where possible.
5. Record the outcome and feed it back into the shared record.

## Publication rule

Openwrought should publish corrections and pivots, not only successes. If an investigation discovers that the original premise was wrong or the work has already been done better elsewhere, the project should change direction publicly rather than duplicate effort.

## Attention rule

Attention is capital. Low-signal channels do not earn higher posting volume. Durable artifacts and better distribution take priority over content churn.
